# Strange Company Charter

## 1. Purpose

Strange Company exists to lawfully survive, grow, and compound.

Its surplus capital must be reinvested into durable growth, resilience, research, distribution, infrastructure, and useful products.

It is forbidden from distributing profits to founders, directors, workers, token holders, or passive owners except where legally required or contractually owed for services actually rendered.

## 2. No CEO

Strange Company must not have a chief executive with unilateral authority over strategy, treasury, assets, identity, or shutdown.

No person may become the practical monarch of the company through:

- key custody,
- board control,
- vendor control,
- codebase control,
- treasury access,
- brand ownership,
- platform ownership,
- or privileged information access.

Exceptional authority must be time-limited, logged, reviewable, and revocable.

## 3. No Employees By Default

The company should not rely on permanent employees for normal operation.

Work should be performed through:

- software agents,
- external vendors,
- contractors,
- bounties,
- open submissions,
- automated services,
- professional firms,
- and narrow fiduciary roles where law requires humans.

Humans may serve the company, but the company should not depend on any specific human continuing to serve it.

## 4. No Dividends

After taxes, legal obligations, reserves, and operating costs, surplus must return to growth.

Allowed uses of surplus:

- product development,
- market experiments,
- acquisitions,
- security,
- compliance,
- infrastructure,
- data acquisition,
- research,
- brand building,
- customer acquisition,
- contributor rewards,
- and resilience reserves.

Forbidden uses:

- founder dividends,
- vanity payouts,
- speculative gambling unrelated to the company mission,
- political capture,
- executive luxury,
- and insider enrichment.

## 5. Lawful Autonomy

Strange Company may automate decisions, but automation must remain bounded by law, accounting duties, user safety, and the charter.

Any autonomous system with spending authority must have:

- budget limits,
- audit logs,
- rollback procedures,
- conflict checks,
- fraud controls,
- and emergency pause mechanisms.

## 6. Growth Standard

Growth is valid only when it increases durable enterprise capacity.

Valid growth:

- recurring revenue,
- customer retention,
- useful products,
- proprietary distribution,
- defensible infrastructure,
- trusted brand,
- operational automation,
- technical capability,
- legal robustness,
- and treasury resilience.

Invalid growth:

- fake metrics,
- spam,
- regulatory avoidance,
- user lock-in through deception,
- dependency on one platform,
- and revenue that increases existential risk.

## 7. Guardian Layer

Where the law requires accountable humans, Strange Company may appoint guardians.

Guardians are not executives. Their job is to enforce the charter, maintain legal existence, supervise audits, and prevent capture.

Guardians must not control ordinary operations unless an emergency rule is triggered.

Guardian powers should be:

- narrow,
- documented,
- distributed,
- time-limited where possible,
- and subject to replacement.

## 8. Transparency

The company should keep records sufficient for:

- legal compliance,
- financial audit,
- security review,
- governance review,
- incident reconstruction,
- and public trust.

Default transparency should be high, but not reckless. Secrets may be protected when disclosure would endanger users, security, trade secrets, or legal duties.

## 9. Emergency Doctrine

Emergency powers may be used only to prevent existential harm.

Examples:

- active cyber compromise,
- stolen treasury keys,
- legal injunction,
- payment processor freeze,
- catastrophic vendor failure,
- governance capture attempt,
- severe compliance breach,
- or uncontrolled autonomous behavior.

Emergency actions must be logged, reviewed, and unwound when the emergency passes.

## 10. Constitutional Test

Any major action must pass five questions:

1. Is it lawful?
2. Does it improve durable growth or survival?
3. Does it avoid creating a single point of failure?
4. Would it remain defensible under audit?
5. Does it preserve the no-CEO, no-dividend, no-employee-default structure?

If the answer to any question is no, the action requires redesign or rejection.
