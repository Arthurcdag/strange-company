# Satellite Company

The satellite company is the profit-facing sister business. The current execution path is Brazil-first.

Strange Company stays sealed: no CEO, no employees, no dividends, and no direct profit extraction. The satellite company is different. It is a normal for-profit vendor that can sell services, keep profit, pay taxes, and fund its own growth.

## Why It Exists

The sealed company is designed to compound and survive. That makes it hard to use as a personal income vehicle.

The satellite solves that by creating a separate business that profits from useful work around the ecosystem without violating the Strange Company charter.

The clean version:

1. The satellite sells to external customers first.
2. It proves market demand without needing Strange Company money.
3. It may later sell services to Strange Company only at market price.
4. Strange Company must be able to choose another vendor.
5. All related-party work is disclosed, invoiced, and reviewable.

## First Offer Stack

The current model uses a low-complexity service mix:

| Offer | Buyer | Price | Cost | Default count | Default profit |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Compliance proof sprint | External clinics | R$750/mo | R$210/mo | 6 | R$3,240/mo |
| Compliance template pack | External buyers | R$79/mo | R$8/mo | 12 | R$852/mo |
| Research Gate maintenance | Related-party contract | R$1,200/mo | R$360/mo | 0 | R$0 |
| Bounty admin desk | Related-party contract | R$900/mo | R$240/mo | 0 | R$0 |

Default target:

```text
R$3,500/month net profit before related-party work is needed.
```

With the default external mix, the satellite can model about `R$4,092/month` net profit. It is still blocked from real launch until the Brazil transaction, tax, privacy, payment, and support controls are closed.

## Profit Readiness

The private Operations tab now includes a `Profit readiness` panel. It answers one concrete question: can Strange Works Studio sell today without pretending the sealed Strange Company is live?

The panel checks four gates:

- external setup: launch checklist, Brazil setup evidence, and critical commercial controls are closed,
- public intake config: support inbox, LGPD contact, Sheet/Form route, payment route, terms, and privacy are configured,
- qualified customer pipeline: at least one private lead is qualified or already moving through the invoice path,
- operational order lifecycle: services exist and the daily pilot run is not paused by a stop rule.

It also shows the current external-only profit model, collected MRR from Operations, and how many external compliance proof sprint customers are still needed to reach `R$3K` and `R$5K` monthly net profit. Related-party work never counts as market proof in this tracker.

## Revenue Start

The Operations console now includes a **Revenue start** board that starts both company lanes without merging them.

The Strange Company lane confirms the sealed boundary, Online Gate review, treasury hold, and receipt-chain baseline. The second company lane confirms Strange Works Studio Brazil setup evidence, first qualified lead, manual payment/invoice route, NFS-e or receipt path, daily pilot run, and delivery closeout.

The start packet is the handoff between readiness and first revenue work. It lets the satellite begin the manual paid pilot while Strange Company stays sealed and does not invoice customers directly.

## Clean Transaction Controls

The satellite may go online as a paid operator only when these controls are clear:

- external customer revenue exists,
- market pricing evidence exists,
- written scope and deliverables exist,
- invoices and bookkeeping lane exist,
- Brazilian tax/NFS-e or fiscal receipt route exists,
- LGPD contact and privacy request process exist,
- conflict disclosure review exists,
- Strange Company has a replaceable-vendor rule.

Related-party revenue is allowed only after the satellite can stand on external customers.

## What It Can Sell To Strange Company

Allowed services:

- Research Gate maintenance,
- bounty desk administration,
- compliance packet production,
- vendor coordination,
- public status and support operations,
- documentation and receipt-chain packaging.

Forbidden patterns:

- arbitrary management fees,
- inflated pricing,
- exclusive vendor lock-in,
- hidden ownership or hidden control,
- moving Strange Company surplus to people without a real service,
- using the sealed company as a laundering route for profit.

## Online Rule

The satellite can go online earlier than Strange Company live operation, but only as an ordinary for-profit service business.

It should start with:

1. public static page,
2. manual payment requests or invoices,
3. terms and privacy copy,
4. support inbox,
5. bookkeeping ledger,
6. two to six external customers,
7. no related-party revenue counted as proof.

Strange Company can be publicly visible as a static prototype while still not operating live. The satellite can be commercially active once its own Brazilian legal, tax, NFS-e/receipt, payment, accounting, privacy, and support gates are ready.

## Operational Loop

The command center now includes an Operations tab.

That tab is the first functional operating surface:

- private paid-pilot lead pipeline,
- customer order intake,
- invoice/payment packet generation,
- local invoice and delivery ledger,
- commercial readiness controls,
- receipt-chain events for orders and operations state.

The prototype blocks an order from moving from `Sent` to `Paid` until the critical commercial controls are marked clear. This is a software guardrail, not a substitute for actually confirming the Brazilian entity/CNPJ route, tax regime, NFS-e/receipt path, bank/payment route, support inbox, LGPD contact, and bookkeeping process.
