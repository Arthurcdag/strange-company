# Strange Company Launch Plan

## Goal

Launch the smallest lawful version of Strange Company that can earn revenue, reinvest surplus, and prove the operating model.

The v0 target is not full autonomy. The v0 target is a working company with increasing automation and decreasing dependence on any single person.

## Phase 0: Design The Container

Duration: 1 to 2 weeks.

Outputs:

- choose public name,
- draft charter,
- pick legal structure with counsel,
- define treasury policy,
- define guardian role,
- define spending thresholds,
- create basic accounting system,
- create security baseline.

Decisions:

- Use **Strange Company** publicly.
- Use **H.A.T.E.** internally for the treasury engine.
- Avoid **Nightmare Company** as a legal or customer-facing name.

## Phase 1: Build The Operating Core

Duration: 2 to 4 weeks.

Build:

- website,
- public charter page,
- payment account,
- accounting workflow,
- task bounty template,
- contractor agreement template,
- product experiment template,
- monthly treasury report format,
- internal decision log,
- basic admin dashboard.

Minimum automation:

- revenue tracking,
- expense classification,
- experiment logging,
- uptime monitoring,
- backup monitoring,
- security alerts.

## Online Gate

Before any public deployment, run the Online Gate.

The order is:

```text
Offline prototype -> Private sandbox -> Public beta -> Live operation
```

The current prototype should be treated as local/offline until the private sandbox launch packet is delivered.

Private sandbox means private access only. It does not mean real-money autonomous operation.

Live operation requires external legal, accounting, privacy, support, and incident-response review.

## Phase 2: Launch First Product

Duration: 30 days.

Pick one narrow paid tool.

Good first product profile:

- B2B buyer.
- Low legal risk.
- Simple recurring workflow.
- Clear before/after value.
- Can charge from day one.
- Can be supported by documentation and async support.
- Can be built with off-the-shelf infrastructure.

Recommended candidate:

**Compliance task tracker for tiny regulated businesses.**

Example niches:

- small dental offices,
- private clinics,
- local contractors,
- salons with licensing requirements,
- short-term rental operators,
- small food businesses,
- bookkeeping firms serving one vertical.

Why this is a good fit:

- recurring pain,
- willingness to pay,
- boring enough to be defensible,
- useful without hype,
- can expand into templates, reminders, document generation, and audit trails.

## Phase 3: Create The Growth Loop

Every month:

1. Review revenue and costs.
2. Allocate treasury by rule.
3. Kill weak experiments.
4. Double down on winners.
5. Launch at least one new experiment.
6. Improve automation.
7. Improve resilience score.
8. Publish a short internal report.

## First 10 Experiments

1. Landing page for one niche compliance tracker.
2. Cold email test to 100 target businesses.
3. $300 search ad test.
4. Manual concierge version before building full SaaS.
5. Paid template pack.
6. Simple reminder dashboard.
7. Document upload and audit trail feature.
8. Referral offer for accountants or consultants.
9. Directory partnership test.
10. Acquire or license a small checklist/database.

## 90-Day Milestones

By day 30:

- legal container chosen,
- charter drafted,
- public site online,
- first product landing page live,
- first 10 customer interviews complete.

By day 60:

- first paid customers,
- basic dashboard live,
- accounting and decision logs working,
- first contractor/bounty task completed,
- first security review complete.

By day 90:

- at least one product with recurring revenue,
- repeatable acquisition channel identified,
- treasury allocation process running,
- one resilience drill completed,
- second product experiment launched.

## Minimum Viable Strange Company

The company becomes real when all of the following are true:

- it has revenue,
- it has written rules for surplus allocation,
- it has no CEO with unilateral authority,
- it uses contractors or vendors instead of employees,
- it records decisions and spending,
- it reinvests surplus,
- it can survive one key person disappearing for 30 days,
- and it has a credible plan to migrate away from any critical vendor.
